# The Problem Space: Inferring Coexponential Session Types from Olivier Actor Behavior

This is a research working document. It states an open problem and does not report a result. Where it describes the inference under study, the design is provisional and the verbs are architectural. Where it cites a precedent that already runs, the verb is present-tense because the precedent ships.

## The question, stated precisely

Given the source text of an Olivier actor behavior, with no session-type annotation written by the developer, how much of the actor's coexponential session type can our Composer reconstruct by analysis alone, and at what point in actor structure does that reconstruction stop being possible, so that the analysis falls back to the wait-for relation it already extracts?

Two parts of that question carry the weight.

The first part is the reconstruction itself. An Olivier actor is written as ordinary state-threading code: a receive loop, a match over a message type, a continuation that hands the next loop iteration an updated state. Nowhere in that code is the conversation typed. The session type, the linear-logic account of who may send what and when over the actor's channels across the lifetime of the client pool, is implicit in the control and data flow of the loop. The reconstruction problem is to recover that implicit type from the explicit behavior, the way our managed-mutability analysis recovers an escape classification from a `let mutable` binding and the way our deadlock-freedom analysis recovers a wait-for rank from a `PostAndReply` call site.

The second part is the boundary. No analysis recovers the full session type of every actor anyone can write, because the constructs an actor may use, content-based routing, value-carried continuations, runtime-spawned handles, put acyclicity and protocol shape past the line where any decision procedure terminates. So the real question is not whether reconstruction is possible in general. It is where the line falls between the fragment a sound and complete analysis can reconstruct and the fragment where the analysis must downgrade. "The boundary" in this document means that line: the precise frontier between the actor behaviors whose coexponential session type our Composer is designed to reconstruct in full and the actor behaviors for which the reconstruction is designed to stop and hand off to the wait-for floor.

Mapping that boundary is the research. It is the same shape of question our [deadlock-freedom work](/docs/design/concurrency/deadlock-freedom-as-an-obligation/) answered for the wait-for relation, now asked of a richer object, and there is no reason yet to assume the answer comes as cleanly.

## What a coexponential session type is, and why behavior under-determines it

The connectives this problem is built on are the coexponentials of Qian, Kavvos, and Birkedal (arXiv:2010.13926, ICFP 2021). They type the stateful server: a process that serves an unbounded and dynamically determined pool of clients while threading state from one client interaction to the next. The standard exponential `!A` gives repetition without shared state, because its contraction and weakening produce independent copies. The coexponential gives repetition with state carried forward, which is exactly the Olivier actor that owns an arena and evolves it across requests.

The server coexponential is the greatest fixed point of the operator `H_A(X) = ⊥ & A & (X ⅋ X)`, and it satisfies the isomorphism

```
¡A ≅ ⊥ & A & (¡A ⅋ ¡A)
```

which unfolds a server into a choice among three behaviors: terminate, serve one more client with interface `A`, or split into two server continuations that each carry the state forward. The dual connective is the client pool, the least fixed point `¿A ≅ μK_A` of `K_A(X) = 1 ⊕ A ⊕ (X ⊗ X)`, and the two are De Morgan duals, `(¡A)⊥ = ¿(A⊥)`. On the client-pool side the structural operations are costructural: `QueW` is coweakening, the empty pool, and `QueA` is cocontraction, absorbing a client into the pool. The connective therefore has costructural rules, distributed weakening and contraction on the pool, and that is a fact about the calculus, not a property our framework adds. Cut-elimination is established for the calculus.

One feature of the dynamics is load-bearing for everything below. The reduction is deliberately nondeterministic. The authors note that the list-shaped formation rules are confluent but unsatisfactory, so QKB quotients client-formation derivations up to permutation, which lets cut elimination serve any constituent client of the pool. The headline example is a racy compare-and-set register: two clients race to update one server's state, and the calculus does not pick a winner. This nondeterminism is operational. It lives in the reduction relation quotiented up to permutation, in the manner of the pi-calculus, and it is not a sum-valued normal form. That is why the connective sits in classical linear logic (CLL), not its differential extension: the DiLL boundary rests on the calculus being Mix-free and having no sum-valued semantics, and this calculus is both, the costructural rules and the operational nondeterminism notwithstanding. A coexponential is a modality, so it is not in MALL, and it is wrong to call it "the linear fragment."

Now the under-determination. The reason behavior does not pin down the session type is that the calculus is built to leave the client-serving order open, and the actor source is built to leave it open too. The state-threading loop of an Olivier actor commits to handling whatever its mailbox delivers next, in mailbox order, and that order is a property of the run, not of the text. The same source serves a different client sequence on every execution. So two distinct session-level behaviors, two different witnesses to which client the pool serves next, are both consistent with one actor source, and the source by itself cannot distinguish them. The session type our Composer is designed to reconstruct names the conversation up to that permutation, which means the inference is reconstructing a quotient, not a single linear order, and the quotient is precisely the operational nondeterminism the calculus quotients over. The behavior under-determines the type in exactly the place the type is built to be permutation-quotiented, and an inference that pretended otherwise would be reconstructing a fiction.

## The specific hardness sources

Four constructs put actor behavior past the line. Each is a thing a working Olivier program does, not a pathological case, and each is named in the wait-for design as a reason a call site lands in its `Unresolved` class.

**Content-based routing.** When an actor chooses its next callee from the content of a message, the routing target is a function of runtime data, and the set of conversations the actor can conduct is a function of the values that flow through it. The session type over such routing is a union taken at the value level, and the analysis cannot enumerate the branches from the text because the branch selector is not in the text. The wait-for analysis already over-approximates this case by emitting a may-wait edge to each possible callee, which is sound for acyclicity. The session type wants more than the wait-for edge: it wants the protocol of each branch and the condition that selects it, and the selecting condition is the runtime value the static text does not hold.

**Value-carried continuations and `Actor.self()`-passing.** An Olivier client passes `Actor.self()` as the reply channel, so the continuation that completes a synchronous round trip is a value that flows through the message, not a statically pinned channel. When a continuation is a value, the session it belongs to is determined by where that value ends up, and where the value ends up is a dataflow question over channels carried in messages. The session type of an actor that receives a channel in a message and later sends on it depends on the provenance of that channel, which the analysis must track through the message payloads. This is the construct that makes the session-typing problem a higher-order one, and it is the same construct the wait-for design flags as value-carried in its `Unresolved of routing` case.

**Runtime-spawned handles.** Olivier spawns actors at runtime, and a spawned actor's reference is a value the spawning code holds and may pass on. The identity of a callee reached through a spawned handle is not statically pinned, so the connection graph is not statically known, and the client pool the server's coexponential ranges over is a pool whose membership is decided at run time. The fixed-point structure of `¡A` is designed for exactly an unbounded, dynamically determined pool, so the connective accommodates this case semantically. The inference does not get the same accommodation for free: it has to recover the pool's shape from spawn sites whose results are first-class values.

**Nondeterministic client-serving order.** This is the hardness source the calculus itself introduces, and it is the one the inference cannot remove by being cleverer, because it is not an imprecision in the analysis. It is a feature of the object being reconstructed. The Olivier mailbox realizes one scheduling of the client race per run, and the session type our Composer is designed to reconstruct is the permutation-quotient over those schedulings, the same quotient QKB take over client formation. An inference for this object is inferring a type modulo permutation, and the soundness condition is that every realizable mailbox scheduling is a member of the reconstructed quotient. This is a different and harder soundness statement than the wait-for analysis carries, because the wait-for analysis only has to over-approximate a set of edges, while the session inference has to characterize a set of admissible orderings without claiming an ordering no run can produce and without omitting one a run can.

These four do not stack into one wall. They are distinct sources, and a fragment that excludes the first three may still have to confront the fourth, because the fourth is intrinsic to the connective. The mapping work has to treat them separately and find the largest fragment that controls all four at once.

## Relationship to the undecidability the wait-for work documents

The wait-for design states the relevant undecidability plainly: acyclicity over value-carried routing is undecidable in general, which is the wall the asynchronous-and-cyclic process-network literature documents and works around. That design responds by drawing a fragment, the statically resolvable callees, where the wait-for relation is a finite directed graph and acyclicity reduces to a strongly-connected-component pass and a QF_LIA rank constraint. Outside the fragment, the call site is classified `Unresolved`, the compiler names the call that dropped out, and that call falls back to supervised execution with a timeout. The guarantee has a visible boundary, and the boundary is the decidability frontier of the wait-for question.

The session-inference problem inherits that undecidability and adds to it. The wait-for relation is a projection of the session type onto a single question, does this actor wait on that one, and the session type is the fuller object the projection is taken from. Reconstructing the fuller object is at least as hard as reconstructing the projection, because the projection is recoverable from the full type but not conversely. So every construct that makes the wait-for question undecidable, the value-carried routing first among them, makes the session-inference question at least as undecidable, and the costructural and nondeterministic structure of the coexponential adds reconstruction obligations the wait-for question never had to carry. There is no expectation that the session-inference boundary coincides with the wait-for boundary. The honest prior is that the session-inference boundary sits inside the wait-for boundary, reconstructing the full type on a strictly smaller fragment than the one on which the wait-for slice succeeds, and that the gap between the two is where the floor matters.

The floor is the design discipline that keeps the harder problem from weakening the shipped guarantee. The wait-for analysis is sound and implemented today. Whatever the session inference can and cannot reconstruct, the wait-for slice remains available as the liveness floor, so declining to reconstruct the full session type on some actor never removes the deadlock-freedom guarantee the wait-for analysis already provides on that actor. The session inference is designed to be strictly additive over the floor: where it succeeds, it supplies the protocol layer; where it does not, the wait-for rank stands unchanged. This is the same relationship the three-layer contract states between the protocol layer and the liveness slice, restated here as the property the boundary-mapping research must preserve at every step.

## Where the decidability discipline of the framework bears on this

The framework's whole game is staying inside fragments where inference is complete, principal, and decidable, the QF_LIA and abelian sweet spot the four-tier architecture is built around, the decidable-by-construction posture. Type checking is decidable given the term. Inference and proof search are undecidable in general, and the framework earns its guarantees by living in fragments where they are not. Free theorems are a Tier 1 phenomenon only, from parametricity over the dimensional and grade structure, with no proof code. Tier 2 is QF_LIA and QF_BV bounds, ranks, and lattice obligations, discharged by Z3. Tier 3 is restricted probabilistic reasoning over the abelian carrier, Z3 plus library lemmas. Tier 4 is pRHL relational, the type checker against a Rocq library with Z3 left as the leaf solver. Z3 alone is trusted through Tier 3, and Rocq enters only at Tier 4.

The session-inference problem is a member of that family of questions, asked at the protocol layer. The protocol shape, where it can be reconstructed, is a Tier-1-in-character property: the session type is structure, protocol fidelity is the content it entails, and the typing derivation is the proof, established once over the type system and not dispatched per program to a solver. Invariants the server maintains across requests are ordinary Tier 2 obligations. Probabilistic and relational guarantees are reached by the existing mode shifts to Tier 3 and Tier 4. None of this moves the decidability of any tier, and the inference does not get to. What the inference has to find is the fragment of actor behavior on which the reconstruction is itself complete, principal, and decidable, the protocol-layer analogue of the QF_LIA sweet spot the rest of the architecture lives in. The boundary this document defines is that fragment's edge. The research is to locate it.

## The honest stance: building it may be how we find out

There is a real possibility that the question cannot be answered on paper. The coexponential calculus is recent, its cut-elimination established in 2021, and its inference is not a solved problem in the literature. The constructs that make Olivier actors useful, content-based routing, value-carried continuations, runtime-spawned handles, are the same constructs that put the session type past the reach of a closed-form decision procedure, and the nondeterministic client-serving order is built into the object being reconstructed. Whether a useful fragment exists, one large enough to cover the actors people actually write and small enough to admit complete, principal, decidable reconstruction, is not something this document can assert, and it may not be something any document can assert in advance of an implementation.

So the working stance is that the Fidelity Framework itself may need to become the expression mechanism that validates whether this inference is feasible. The constructs that define the boundary, the actor receive loop, `Actor.self()`-passing, runtime spawn, mailbox scheduling, are Olivier and Composer constructs, and the carriers the inference would ride, the joint-constraint axis of the PHG, the PSG, the tier ladder, the BAREWire wire format, the coeffect system, are our own. The cheapest faithful model of the problem is the framework expressing it. Building the inference inside our Composer, on our actors, against our tiers, is plausibly the only instrument precise enough to find the boundary, because the boundary is a property of how these specific constructs interact, and an abstract calculus model would have to reconstruct the framework to study them. Building it is part of finding out whether it can be built. That is the provisional register this scaffold is written in, and the demonstrated-versus-designed line stays absolute throughout: the wait-for analyzer and the HelloArty width and budget inference are shipped precedents and may be spoken of in the present tense, and the coexponential session inference is a design target spoken of as one until an implementation says otherwise.

## What this document fixes for the rest of the scaffold

The question is fixed: how much of an Olivier actor's coexponential session type can our Composer reconstruct from behavior before the wait-for floor takes over. The object is fixed: the coexponential of QKB, server `¡A` as the greatest fixed point of `H_A`, client pool `¿A` as the least fixed point of `K_A`, duals, costructural on the pool, operationally nondeterministic, in CLL and not DiLL, Mix-free with no sum-valued semantics. The hardness sources are fixed: content-based routing, value-carried continuations and `self()`-passing, runtime-spawned handles, and the intrinsic client-serving nondeterminism. The relationship to the wait-for undecidability is fixed: the session boundary sits inside the wait-for boundary, and the wait-for slice is the floor that the inference is additive over and never weakens. And the stance is fixed: the boundary is the deliverable, the framework may have to express the problem to validate it, and nothing in this scaffold describes the inference as a present fact.
